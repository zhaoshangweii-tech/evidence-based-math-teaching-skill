# Official Evidence Workflow

## Establish the source hierarchy

Use sources in this order unless the task requires a different authority:

1. National curriculum standards and official interpretations.
2. National or provincial official textbook platforms.
3. Publisher textbooks and teacher guides.
4. Official teaching-research publications.
5. Secondary lesson plans, articles, and commercial resources.

Never use a lower-level source to override a higher-level source without explaining the conflict.

## Define the edition precisely

Record:

- publisher or edition,
- grade and semester,
- platform title,
- revision label shown by the platform,
- retrieval date,
- textbook print page and platform resource page when both exist.

Use "platform current edition" when the platform does not explicitly label a volume as revised. Do not convert the current calendar year into a textbook-edition label.

## Search systematically

For cross-edition research:

1. Enumerate all editions available on the selected official platform.
2. Enumerate the relevant grades and volumes.
3. Search direct topic terms and semantic neighbors.
4. Review contents and likely units even when OCR produces no hit.
5. Classify each hit by instructional role:
   - dedicated lesson or project,
   - direct exercise set,
   - quantitative application,
   - contextual mention only.
6. Save representative original pages.

Do not stop after finding the first familiar edition.

## Handle scanned and image-based files

- Render pages at readable resolution.
- Use OCR as a discovery aid, not as final evidence.
- Visually verify names, page numbers, mathematical symbols, diagrams, and tables.
- Preserve the original file and record the page mapping.
- Keep an OCR-review table when processing many volumes or pages.

## Maintain a source ledger

Use a table with these fields:

|Field|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Source ID|Stable short identifier|
|Authority|Standard, platform, textbook, teacher guide, or secondary source|
|Edition|Publisher, grade, semester, revision status|
|Location|URL, file path, print page, platform page|
|Evidence|What the page directly establishes|
|Use|Which conclusion or slide uses it|
|Checked|Date and reviewer status|

## Write evidence-aware conclusions

Use formulations such as:

- "The official textbook page directly shows..."
- "Across the editions reviewed on the platform..."
- "This suggests an instructional emphasis on..."
- "The student drawing is consistent with..., but an interview is needed to confirm..."

Avoid:

- "All editions..."
- "Students think..."
- "The newest 2026 edition..."

unless the source scope and evidence fully support the statement.
